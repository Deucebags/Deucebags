Privacy

Deucebags has no ads or tracking, and accounts are optional. Here's exactly what it stores and where.

What stays on your phone

Your scores, results, players, league, achievements, profile and friends list are saved inside the app on your phone. Nothing is uploaded to us or anyone else. Deleting the app or clearing your browser's data removes it.

Videos and photos

The camera and microphone are only used while you're filming or scanning a friend code. Videos and clips stay in the app until you save or delete them. When you save, a copy goes to your phone's own storage. Your profile photo is saved on your phone only.

What you choose to share

Friend codes and match results travel inside the links you send. Anyone with one of those links can see what's in it: your name, photo, bio and socials for a friend code, or the match score for a result. Nothing is shared unless you send it.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional. If you do, your results, profile and friends list are also saved to your Deucebags account on Google Firebase, stored in London, so they sync between your devices. Your results stay private to you, apart from matches you play with friends you've connected with, which are sent to them. Your profile card (name, username, photo, bio, padel details and headline stats such as matches played, win rate, rating and achievements) can be seen by other Deucebags users who search for your username. If you join a shared group, matches tagged with that group, and live scores while you're scoring one, are shared with its members. Your email is used to sign you in and is never shown to anyone. You can delete your account and everything in it at any time from Account and sync.

Other services the app uses

The app is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads its fonts from Google Fonts and its QR code tool from Cloudflare. Like any website, those services can see your device's IP address when the app loads. They don't receive any of your scores, videos or profile.

How ratings work

Everyone starts on 1,000. Your rating goes up when you win and down when you lose. How far it moves depends on who you played.

Who you play matters

Your pair's strength is the average of your two ratings. Before each match the app works out how likely each pair was to win. Beat a stronger pair and you gain a lot. Beat a weaker pair and you gain a little. Losing works the same way in reverse.

Some examples

Two pairs both on 1,000: the winners gain 16 and the losers drop 16.

You're on 1,000 and beat a pair on 1,200: you gain about 24. Lose to them and you only drop about 8, because you weren't expected to win.

The most anyone can gain or lose from one match is 32.

Fair for everyone

Both players in a pair move by the same amount, and the other pair moves the opposite way, so points are swapped, never created. A single rotation set counts for half a match. Changing partners doesn't matter, because everyone has their own rating.

Padel rules, the short version

Scoring

Points go 15, 30, 40, game. At 40–40 your group decides: advantage (win two points in a row), golden point (next point wins) or star point (two advantages, then a deciding point). Six games wins a set, but you need to be two clear. At 6–6 there's a tiebreak to 7, again two clear.

Serving

Bounce the ball behind the service line, then hit it at or below waist height with at least one foot on the ground. Serve diagonally into the opposite service box. It must bounce in the box before it touches any wall. You get two attempts.

If the serve clips the net and lands in the box, replay it. If it then hits the metal fence, it's a fault.

Who serves

Teams swap serve every game, and partners take turns, so all four players serve in rotation. At the start of each set, a pair can change which of them serves first.

The walls

The ball must bounce on the ground on the other side before touching any wall or fence. After that bounce, it can hit the walls and you can still play it before it bounces a second time.

You can hit the ball against your own back or side glass to get it over the net. If your shot hits their wall or fence before bouncing, it's your point lost.

You lose the point if

The ball bounces twice on your side, you hit it into your own fence so it can't come back, you touch the net, or the ball hits you or your partner.

Changing ends

Swap sides after the first game and then every two games (after games 1, 3, 5 and so on). In a tiebreak, swap every six points. Deucebags tells you when.

These are the basics most clubs play by. Local rules can vary, so agree them before you start.
